A word cloud turns a pile of text into a picture you can read at a glance. The
bigger a word appears, the more often it showed up, or the more weight someone
decided it deserves. That second half matters: raw frequency alone flatters
filler words and buries the terms that actually carry the message.

Word clouds are quick, honest summaries when the words are chosen carefully, and
decorative noise when they are not. Strip the filler, merge the duplicates, keep
the terms a reader would recognise, and the cloud starts to say something. Give
every term a weight next to its count and the cloud starts to argue something.

Text goes in, a picture comes out. The picture is only ever as good as the text.
